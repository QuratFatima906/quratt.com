## The problem

People underestimate where their time goes, and most time trackers answer with goals, streaks and a score. Tocky's brief was the opposite: **your day has receipts, and nobody is going to tell you off about them.**

## What I built

An iOS-first time tracker. An owl with five expressions does the talking.

- **One-tap timer.** Pick a category, start, pause, resume, stop. The time is worked out from when the session started, ended and paused, never counted second by second, so it stays right even when iOS puts the app to sleep.
- **Home: today at a glance.** What ran today, split by category, with the session in progress pinned on top.
- **History.** Every session grouped by day, and each one can be opened to fix its times, category, note or linked task.
- **Insights.** A weekly chart of where the hours went, compared with last week as a plain observation rather than a score.
- **Tasks.** A short list of things to do, which a session can be linked to so the time lands against the work.
- **Your categories.** Add your own, archive the ones you stop using and restore them later. Light and dark mode both work.
- **Export and reminders.** Everything leaves the app as CSV or JSON with the same durations the screens show, and an optional daily nudge reminds you to log, without a target to miss.
- **Private by default.** Everything is kept in SQLite on the phone, with no account and no network calls when starting or stopping a timer.
- **It catches its own mistakes.** A timer left running overnight, or one that starts in the future because the clock moved, is flagged when you open the app, and a save that fails says so instead of pretending.
